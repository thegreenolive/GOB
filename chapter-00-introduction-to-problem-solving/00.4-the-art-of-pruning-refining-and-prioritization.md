# 00.4 The Art of Pruning, Refining, and Prioritization&#x20;

After laying out the problem's intrinsics with a diagram, Lucy can focus on aspects that could yield a greater positive impact on solving the issue. Pruning a problem’s tree diagram helps focus attention on the problem's important elements. Gathering evidence from Lucy and using some detective skills, the problematic process will surface as two options: 1) Lucy is not doing a good job of waking up and getting ready, or 2) The public transportation is having issues.

Lucy knows that the bus is generally on time and has consistent commute times (delays and traffic have little impact on this route).  Therefore, the culprit in this exercise is Lucy’s daily routine. Of course, this conclusion is obvious to Lucy, since she usually misses the 6:45 AM bus; the reason is that she is late, not that the bus is early or late.  After identifying the process that needs attention, Lucy can analyze further and make the necessary corrections. Now, some problems may not have a single cause. A scenario could be that Lucy is sometimes late at the bus stop, and sometimes the delay is due to the commute. In those cases, the stakeholder must decide where to focus first.

Lucy’s problem seems to be caused by her daily morning routine. As the name of the step indicates, there are two components in this stage: 1) waking up and 2) getting ready to leave. These two elements must be bonded and meet the set deadline for Lucy to leave by 7:05 AM (so he can catch the 7:15 AM bus).

After improving our understanding of a problem, it is useful to redefine the problem space to accommodate factors that may have been hidden before. The problem to solve is the same, but the understanding of it has changed.  A new diagram analyzing Lucy's process of waking up and getting ready is shown in Fig. 4.

All the diagrams created to analyze Lucy’s problem have helped to identify what _not_ to work on. The analyst must always try to create diagrams that follow the MECE principle. Nevertheless, once a Tree diagram has been created, not every branch deserves analysis. This is the core of the concept of "Pruning”: cutting off branches of the tree that have little impact or are hard to influence. In the future, this concept will be used to avoid writing code for features that don't solve the core problem.
