# 03.4 Walkthroughs

In this section, students will use walk-throughs to review the steps explained in the previous section. A walk-through is a systematic process used to verify the logic of a solution before or during its implementation in a computer environment. Walkthroughs simulate some of the resources available to programmers when actually programming a solution, but seek to expand understanding of how to plan the coding.

This process begins with a human level walk-through (humanual), where a person uses pencil and paper (the example uses a spreadsheet-like tool) to solve the problem manually. By first identifying the input and expected output, the designer ensures the logic is general enough to handle various scenarios without relying on anomalies or shortcuts.

The next level is the program level walk-through. In this phase, in addition to the inputs and outputs, all “memory slots” used to assist computations are specified. The complexity of a walk-through increases from the human level to the program level. Finally, a deeper walkthrough covering the hardware components and how they perform fundamental CPU-level operations is the next level, but this type will not be covered in this guide.
