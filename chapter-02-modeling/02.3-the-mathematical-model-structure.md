# 02.3 The Mathematical Model Structure

Mathematical models are well-suited for programming because their structure is well-defined. To illustrate this, this section will use a practical example. The company $${pi}$$ bakes and sells frozen pies. Each pie is sold for $20, and the cost of crafting one pie is $4.4. Additionally, there are indirect costs associated with shipping pies due to the cost of the isolated boxes. The cost of an isolated box is proportional to the square of the number of pies it contains; the exact shipping cost in dollars is 0.3 times (number of pies)<sup>2</sup>. Intuitively, the more pies that are baked, shipped, and sold, the more money. Nevertheless, the cost grows exponentially, and the return grows linearly. For example, for 100 pies, the cost (crafting and shipping) is $3,440, while the sale price is only $2,000; selling more pies results in a loss! The problem is to find what is the number of pies that must be sold to maximize profits.

According to the mathematical model, selling more or fewer than 26 pies will decrease the operation's profits. Please note that this model doesn't capture the full reality of the pie business. There are many factors that are not included in the model. Competition, season, location, and even weather can introduce fluctuations and completely change the model's outcome.

A formal mathematical model consists of several specific components:

* **Parameters**: These are the known, fixed values in a problem, such as the selling price per item and costs.
* **Variables**: These are the values we need to determine. In the given example, it is the operation's profit.
* **Objective Function**: A formula that represents the primary goal. In the example, the objective function is the equation.
* **Resource Constraints**: These are limits on what can be done, such as the amount of available raw material. If there were a limit on the number of pies that could be baked per day (e.g., the oven could bake at most 20 pies per day), that number could be considered a constraint.
