# 02.1 What is a model?

In the first section of this guide, it was advised to use the MECE principle when analyzing a problem. This idea is feasible in practical terms because it assumes a simplification of reality. A model is an abstraction of a situation or circumstance that omits nonessential details and provides a functional representation of what matters.

The role of models is diverse. Some models are used to make predictions or find solutions, while in some cases, models help explain phenomena. There are experiments that, due to costs or risks, cannot be reproduced in real life, so the conditions are applied to a model instead.

Be aware that a model is NOT reality, but a good approximation to it. The quality of the approximation depends on the requirements of the task, and usually the better the more complexity is added to the model. Again, a model aids in understanding what reality may be, but because it cannot capture reality, it is, by definition, inaccurate. As George E. P. Box famously stated, “All models are wrong, but some are useful”.
