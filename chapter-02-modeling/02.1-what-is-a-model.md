# 02.1 What is a model?

In the first section of this guide, it was advised to use the MECE principle when analyzing a problem. This idea is feasible in practical terms because it assumes a simplification of reality. A model is an abstraction of a situation or circumstance that omits nonessential details and provides a functional representation of what matters.

Be aware that a model is NOT reality, but a good approximation that may facilitate the understanding or solution development of a problem/situation. Most of the time, models are used to make predictions or find solutions, but in some cases, they also help explain a phenomenon. Again, a model aids in understanding what reality may be, but because it cannot capture reality, it is, by definition, inaccurate. As George E. P. Box famously stated, “All models are wrong, but some are useful”.
