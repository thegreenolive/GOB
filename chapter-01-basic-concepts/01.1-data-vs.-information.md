# 01.1 Data vs. Information

The distinction between data and information is the foundation of computer science. Data is usually described as the raw material—the unprocessed facts, figures, and symbols that enter a system. Processed data is usually termed information, which is data that has been organized, structured, or presented in a context to make it useful. While these terms are often used interchangeably in casual conversation, in a technical sense, the computer is a "data processor" that outputs "information". Be aware that the user’s knowledge and background can also determine whether a piece of data is information. For example, after simulating the weather, the program indicates a drop in the barometric pressure, which, for a weather enthusiast, indicates a storm, but for a regular person, it is just data (Figure 01.1).
