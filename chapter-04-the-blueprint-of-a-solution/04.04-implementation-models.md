# 04.04 Implementation Models

Implementation models illustrate the "how" of a solution. They show the computer's low-level perspective and the specific steps required to process data and obtain results. Since this type of model needs to specify decision-making, the notation and "grammar" are more elaborate than those of essential models. There are two types of implementation models considered in this section: **Flowcharts** and **Pseudocode**.&#x20;

### Flowcharts

Flowcharts can be considered "dynamic" diagrams because the audience can follow the program's logic by analyzing the program's flowchart. This methodology uses the following symbols:&#x20;

1. Oval (Termination): Represents the beginning (START) and the end (STOP) of a program. Every structured program should have exactly one of each.
2. Parallelogram (Input/Output): Used for all data entry and output processes, such as "Input num" or "Output sum".
3. Rectangle (Process): Used for arithmetic processes or assignments, such as SUM = SUM + NUM.
4. Diamond (Decision/Selection): Represents a juncture where logic paths diverge based on a condition. It must have at least three flow lines: one entering from the top and at least two leaving (Yes/No).
5. Flow Lines: Lines with arrows that indicate the direction of the logic flow.
